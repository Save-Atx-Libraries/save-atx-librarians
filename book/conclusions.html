After chapters 1–28

What the whole folder supports — and what it does not.

These conclusions come only after the chapter files exist. They are limited to what the captions in this folder actually contain: mostly fall 2025. June 2026 is outside the tapes.

1. The board was not in the schools the way the public was.

Campus nights (ch. 8–14, 17, 19–24) are superintendent, communications, planning, operations. Trustees, when named at all, sit or “stick around” and do not take questions (Sunset Valley: Gonzalez and Kaufman introduced, then silent; Dawson: “you have a trustee in the room,” silent; Maplewood: Hunter will stay after, no Q&A; Becker: Boswell and Kaufman present, silent). The board talks on the dais (ch. 15, 18, 25, 27–28). That is a documented split: input is collected in gyms by staff; decisions are spoken downtown. It is not proof no trustee ever visited a campus. It is proof that in these recordings the board is not the body answering parents in the library.

2. The year was built to feed TEA, not to rewrite the formula.

Staff say 24 TAPs to the agency. Closures are how TAP money (~$800k extra at a combined campus, Bedichek ch. 20) and utilization (76% toward 85%, ch. 6) get scored. Public on Oct 9: avoiding takeover is “number one,” but “closing schools is obeying in advance.” Recapture ($700–715M) is said on the dais (ch. 7, 18) and at Bedichek (ch. 20: $714M + $56M). Inflation catch-up ($1,100–$1,300 vs about $200) is said once, by staff, at Bedichek, not as a motion. Nov 20 (ch. 28): recapture, inflation, and allotment do not appear. Item 14.3 still carries 6–3.

3. Librarians and planning time were the identity script, not the vote.

Sep 25, Oct 9, Sunset Valley, Dawson, Palm, Ridgetop, Oak Springs: we retain / we haven’t reduced / we protect / we love librarians; planning time is “who we are.” Dawson even names librarians as extras the state does not require — optional in the same sentence as a promise. This folder never votes to cut them. The June 2026 cut list is the later break of that script. Maplewood is the tell inside 2025: trading a librarian for a coach was already thinkable.

4. Public asked for two things the process did not give.

A moratorium (ch. 3, Sep 18). Proof from the last round of closures — campus-by-campus savings and enrollment loss — before another yes (ch. 28). Captions: those numbers “will never be forthcoming,” then 14.3 as amended 6–3. Work-session public (ch. 15): closing can cost money once attrition, buses, and recapture are counted. That argument is not answered with a published ledger in these files.

5. What “result” means, night by night.

Campus meetings: no vote. PFC (ch. 5): housing project 4–0, not the closure list. Oct 30 regular (ch. 18): TRAN $19M for payroll; recapture named; closures not on that agenda. Nov 6 (ch. 25): pause broader boundaries; TAPs still aimed at Nov 20. Nov 20 (ch. 28): TAP package mostly 8–1 / 7–2 / 5–4 slices; 14.3 programmatic remaining consolidations 6–3. That is the result of the year in this folder.

What this book cannot do

It cannot hear tone. Names scramble. It cannot see who sat silent off-mic. It cannot substitute for the video zip. It cannot cover spring 2026. If more tapes arrive, they get new chapters — they do not get folded into a gist of these 28.
