Chapter 27 — Public hearing on turnaround plans, November 20, 2025

**Source:** `meetings/clean/20251121-Austin-ISD-Board-of-Trustees-Public-Hearing--November-20-2025.txt` **Clock:** Called to order **[00:00:15]** Thursday, November 20, 2025 at **5:01 p.m.** Adjourned **[02:31:52] at 7:32 p.m.** Then a short break and the regular voting meeting (Chapter 28). **Room:** Board of Trustees, AISD central office. Trustee Gonzalez on Zoom. Hearing is TAP-only; other items wait for the voting meeting. **Four in-person speakers, six recorded.** One minute each. **Caption noise:** Segura / Sigur / Sagura; Widen = Weedane / Wedane / Widane; Winn = when / Win Montasauri; Barrington = Bington; Bedichek = Betachch; Pecan Springs = Pan Springs.

This is the legally required hearing before TAP submission. Speakers are mostly Widen restart vs. close. Staff then repeats the six TEA strategies, the 1882 pause, Burnet/Webb/Doby as year-five campuses, and the campus list. Trustee questions fill more than an hour: TEA will not give more time; TEA success criteria vs. sending students to D campuses; 850 special-education moves; class-size ratios that are “not a legal requirement”; whether a restart at Widen would even keep the teachers families love.

Staff presentation — [00:16:26]–[00:47:22]

Segura introduces Dr. Latanya Emerson (school improvement), Jennifer Pace (elementary leadership), Dr. Angel Wilson (secondary), Dr. Maryanne Maxwell (academics), Brandy Hosek (talent). Concurrent work: TAPs “in compliance with Texas Education Agency requirements,” plus “budget deficits, program realignments, the ever shifting accountability system.”

TAP definition repeated: two unacceptable counts (three consecutive D’s, or F’s); counts back to 2018–19; implement in year three; two years to break the chain before year five. Plans that will not be accepted cannot be submitted; rejection risks “further escalation via state intervention up to installation of a board of managers.”

Six strategies again. Dobie / Burnet / Webb = district-managed restart. Mendez = partner-managed. Closure/reassign to a C or better; TAP assigned to the receiving school. New school must be new scope. At 3 UA, school improvement “unlikely.” Fifth UA: commissioner **shall** close or install a board of managers — “often referred to as a… state takeover.” **Burnet, Webb, and Dobie are currently in their fifth year [00:22:03].**

**1882 contingency [00:22:27].** Partner with demonstrated F-to-C (or better) restart; two-year pause; fifth year can become year one of the pause. Board stated that ability June 26 for Burnet/Webb/Dobie. Not written into every TAP: if a receiving campus (example: **Bedichek TAP assigned to Covington**, which has no UA) carried 1882 language, it would scare staff. Ability exists “regardless if it’s in the plan or not.”

History: chains to 2018–19. ~A dozen schools B to F when STAAR changed 2022–23. 2024–25: more A’s, fewer F’s; he expects F’s down “significantly” this year.

**Emerson [00:25:56].** Lessons from Dobie / Webb / Burnet: HQIM, leadership coaching, PD, strategic staffing, early recruitment, vacancy management, data trackers. Opportunities: planning, staffing, student supports; MTSS; family engagement (dual-capacity, adult ESL, enrollment assistance, wraparound); revised master schedule; expanded electives and interventions; lighthouse; targeted PD. **Middle-school restart schedule:** four-period day to five-period; 90-minute periods to ~70; more weekly minutes; **450 minutes/week ELA and math, 270 science and social studies**; one extended day per week; summer school. Family engagement: Karen Mapp dual-capacity framework.

**Maxwell [00:29:30].** Evaluation table: quantitative left, qualitative right. MAP (BOY/MOY), STAAR interim, curriculum-based assessments, student writing on common rubrics, campus assessments/data trackers, district walkthroughs plus technical-partner walkthroughs, PLCs. MOY MAP projected proficiency **~84–88%** accurate for STAAR, “a little bit more reliable even than the STAAR interim.” MTSS / campus study teams; individualized plans.

**Pace [00:34:44].** Seven closure/reassign: Barrington, Bedichek, Dawson, Oak Springs, Martin, **Winn Montessori, Widen**. Five ACE restarts: Linder, Sanchez, Paredes, Pecan Springs, Wooldridge. Twelve school-improvement: TA Brown, Govalle, Hart, Houston, Jordan, Norman, Sims, Overton, Padron, Pickle, Sadler Means, Wooten, Eastside ECHS (federal CSI label). Strategy chosen for TEA acceptance, enrollment, facilities, proximity, multi-year trends.

Receiving map **[00:36:40]:** Dawson→Galindo; Oak Springs→Blackshear; majority Barrington→Guerrero Thompson; majority Bedichek→Covington; majority Martin→Keeling. **Widen→Rodriguez (currently D, B the previous year).** Majority Winn→**Andrews (D)**; smaller share to Pecan Springs (3 UA restart). Smaller Bedichek share to Paredes (3 UA). Smaller Martin share to Marshall (D). Where students go to a restart or D, “keep the number… manageable.” Extra resources at restart and D receivers.

**Hosek [00:38:46].** 2 UA school improvement: additional staffing allotment, principal discretion. Restarts/reassigns: same plus “professional incentives” (not a stipend — goals attached, paid on achievement), supplemental hours, family supports, discretionary funds. **About $1 million per restart/reassign campus; ~$350,000 per school-improvement campus.** Timeline: tonight’s vote; tomorrow a **preliminary preference survey to all staff districtwide**; December, superintendent selects TAP principals; January **hiring freeze** (including central office) so campus staff can move first; spring match fairs; **external hiring not until April**.

CDA targets: focus domain **2A growth** to a C. Two-year TAP. Eastside ECHS: D, federal CSI (bottom 5% statewide); C target.

Segura close **[00:45:45]:** “state intervention risk”; fewer F schools, more A schools; “the system is responding.”

TAPs exist so the district invests; “the other side of it it’s not really just up to us right TEA approves these plans.” **Widen and Winn:** closure/reassign to D campuses, against TEA written success criteria (A/B/C receiving school). “If I’m voting on this, I have to feel very very like 100% confident… they’re going to accept because this is displacing a lot of people.”

Weekly agency contact from the special-ed agreed-order relationship. Deputy commissioners at times. Comprehensive plan (TAPs + program moves + later boundaries) is what TEA looks at “as a whole.” Rodriguez was a B last year, has a D not a UA. Winn: minority to Pecan Springs (restart); Andrews a D, history and resources to be written into the TAP. Widen originally split Houston (F risk) and Rodriguez; they unsplit after community/trustee feedback. Palm would be farther. “Do we have anything in written form? … that is not how the agency typically works.” Happy to share dates.

Comparison BOY-to-BOY is “a little more difficult.” Deep dive promised December. Quintana — class size at Rodriguez — [01:15:13]–[01:26:26]

“If a school is going to be closed and reassigned… there were should be no more than 20 students in each class… Is that true?” Segura: “It’s not a requirement.” Documents commit to changing student-teacher ratio for restart or 3 UA. Hosek, slide 9: currently staff K–4 at **1:24 with a waiver**; would try **1:20** at 3 UA; middle from **1:29 to 1:25**. Not legal.

Quintana does the math: Widen **342** + Rodriguez **345** = **687**. 27 classrooms × 20 = **540**. “So we’re not going to do the 1:20 ratio.” Hosek: keep it as low as space allows; if out of space, 1:21 or 1:22. Enrollment unknown for fall. Combined special-ed **92 + 83**. Segura: ~**74%** of AISD special-ed students are in general education; 1:10 is life-skills, not the merge. De Urioste: resource, SPS, life-skills each need rooms; combining might force two life-skills rooms the building cannot hold, then centralized placements “we don’t have any prediction of yet.” Staffing “in the spring.” Segura: utilization was dropped at the start of this process to protect those rooms; “without a vote… the detail you want, we’re just not yet able to provide.” Rodriguez “not too long ago was much much larger.” De Urioste: choice becomes move sped or raise gen-ed cap. Segura: they looked at splitting Widen; community and trustees said don’t split and don’t send to an F (Houston, even though modernized). “Now we’re going to a school that wholly can fit.”

**Class size is on this night.** It is TAP implementation math, not a motion, not 22:1 statute, not a cut. Quintana is counting classrooms. Segura is counting surplus seats and TEA.

2 UA: plan now, implement 2026–27. 3 UA: implement as soon as approved (spring 2026) — hiring principals in December is already TAP action. 1882 and closure are **not interchangeable** at year five: students still take STAAR before a closure, so it does not consume the fifth year; 1882 pause does. Pursuing grants so it doesn’t all hit general operating. Hosek: extra staffing frees Title dollars campuses now spend to keep a staff member. Boswell: “lean into our community… can you help us?” Analysis of combining EB / eco-dis / STAAR groups: weighted averages on splits, rebaseline.
