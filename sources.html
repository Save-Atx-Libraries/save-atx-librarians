Sources

Checked against the public file and the caption tracks in this project. Rounded dollars are labeled. If the district’s own tables disagree, the page says so.

The law

  • Senate Bill 7, 73rd Legislature, 1993. Signed May 31, 1993. Legislative Reference Library: effective immediately, with one section on September 1, 1993. Chapter 347.
  • First collections, 1994: 34 districts, $131 million. Texas Tribune, citing the record of that year. An AISD explainer has said $127 million. The page uses $131 million.
  • Now Chapter 49, Education Code. Recapture receipts are Comptroller revenue object 3680, deposited to the Foundation School Fund, not the Economic Stabilization Fund. Texas School Coalition: the payment reduces what the state must contribute.
  • Statewide cumulative since 1994: about $45.5 billion. Texas Tribune, July 24, 2026.

Austin ISD money

  • $715,523,373 adopted recapture, FY2025–26. AISD budget process page. About half of a $1.70 billion adopted expenditure budget. Enrollment used on that page: 72,303.
  • About $8.3 billion, FY2000–01 through FY2024–25. Same AISD pages. The sentence starts in 2000–01 on purpose.
  • $181 million projected 2026–27 operating hole before the June close-out. About $205 million in reductions. About $887 million operating budget adopted June 18, 2026.
  • 48.4% economically disadvantaged, 2025–26. TEA district profile, Austin ISD 227901.

The other fund, and the base dollar

  • $27.5 billion Economic Stabilization Fund, June 30, 2026. Comptroller bond appendix. Cap for 2026–27 given there as $26.9 billion. A forecast table has used $26.5 billion. Either way the balance is over the cap.
  • Basic allotment $6,160 after House Bill 3 (2019), Education Code §48.051. House Bill 2 added $55. The $7,756 figure is $6,160 moved by CPI-U from 2019 to 2025. It is not a statute.

The meetings

  • Caption files of Austin ISD board meetings, December 18, 2025 through June 18, 2026. Vote counts are the chair’s spoken counts on those tracks. The district’s own posted captions supply the April 30 call to order (6:10 p.m.) and the 15 / 16 / 17 percent reserve scenarios, and the May 21 call to order (6:04 p.m.).
  • Budget 7–1–1 and librarian 6–3 are on the June 18, 2026 voting-meeting track. The tape does not name the budget’s no vote or abstention, and it does not name who moved the 6–3. This project’s reading of that track puts Arati Singh on the deputy-superintendent-and-ombudsman try (2–5–2) and Candace Hunter, second LaRessa Quintana, on the salary-only try (3–5–1).
  • Next-day coverage does not use those mover names. Community Impact (June 19, 2026) names Kathryn Whitley Chu on a deputy-superintendent amendment about $477,000 short, and David Kauffman, second Andrew Gonzales, on the $1.1 million fund-balance amendment that passed. The Austin Chronicle (June 25, 2026) also names Kauffman, puts the half-time proposal at $977,500 across 23 schools, and is the source for Eric Ramos, Jessica Meza, the hub-stop figure of 46.5 bus drivers, and the “almost seven hours.” FOX 7 (June 18, 2026) matches the 2–5–2 count without a name, describes a withdrawn $1.1 million wording, and is the source for the contract salary and the deleted campus-rating bonus. The Statesman is the source for 11.5 librarian positions and the June 11 rally photo.
  • June 4 detail — Gabby Garcia, Alexia Haywood’s May 28 letter, Candace Hunter’s hesitation — is the Chronicle’s June 11, 2026 article, not a caption line from June 11. The June 11 caption line is a separate speaker claiming a broken promise on librarians.
  • April 7 dollars ($117–$132 million in scenarios, vacant-position range, three properties) are Community Impact that night. Segura’s April 8 letter is the district’s: $49 million to close the current year, $181 million for 2026–27, about $21 million from the November closures, preliminary budget April 23, vote June 18.
  • May 7 room, Education Austin’s three demands, and the two-year 7-of-8 phase are the Chronicle, May 14, 2026. The $715.5 million recapture comparison in that piece is the 2025–26 adopted figure, not the June 18 budget.

  • November 20–21, 2025 closure roll calls are from KXAN, FOX 7, the Chronicle, and Community Impact. Those meetings are not in the caption folder. The Chronicle’s later “11 schools” is the write-up this site already flags against a 10-campus vote.
