## Opening (`00:00:01`–`00:21:01`)

Host: “superintendent talk over consolidation and closure TAP plans.” Ali: norms, one minute, November 20 vote. Recorded English and Spanish, posted ~24 hours.

Segura why (`00:04:13`): funding (state and federal); changing accountability / local-control risk; enrollment decline; city around a 144-year-old map; cost of living. Five elements: feeder alignment (one ES → ≤2 MS, one MS → ≤2 HS — not true in East Austin or north-central; some MS to 3–5 HS); transfer SOP/policy; **>20,000 empty seats** (“3,000 more than the Moody Center”); consecutive UAs; program alignment (wall-to-wall DL as EB share declines).

Bedichek close then reopen later. TEA must approve what the school will be — not next year (`00:13:21`). Definition in the plan so the community and trustees can hold him, but not so much certainty that community process is skipped.

**Transfer changes vs. October 3** (`00:14:34`): Bedichek transfer students **automatically approved** to whichever of the three receiving schools they choose (including out-of-district, e.g. Kyle → Covington). Rising sixth and ninth graders: boundary changes will **not** control; transfers auto-approved. Numbers work. Over time, rebalance.

Steenport map (`00:16:41`): version 2 Oct. 31. Tiny Lively sliver removed — cleans Williams and St. Elmo splits. Blue → Paredes; green → Covington; purple → Mendez.

TAP (`00:18:15`): follows majority → Covington. Combined “Covington Bedichek” community. December principal. TAP staff requirements; others hiring fairs through April. Consolidation frees building cost for instruction. Each TAP campus (this would be one) **~$800,000** extra — coaches, interventionists, counselors, principal’s call (`00:20:29`).

## Finance exchange (`01:23:05`–`01:27:39`)

**Dylan Vickner**, eighth grader at Bedichek, fourth grader who wants to come (`01:22:52`):

Closing can mean crowded classrooms. Main question:

> considering that in the last year, Austin taxpayers have paid nearly **$700 million** towards the **Robin Hood recapture** plan to fund other school districts throughout the state of Texas… and simultaneously, the state has a **$24 billion** tax surplus. How is it not possible to reallocate some of those funds to help… cover this **$20 million** deficit? (`01:23:16`–`01:23:47`)

**Segura** (`01:23:49`):

> What’s your name?  
> Dylan.  
> So, Dylan, what you’re describing is what the board and I have been fighting for for the last many years. I mean, like we we have.  
> So, the Robin Hood or the recapture, we spent we sent **$714 million about 8 weeks ago**, and then we spent **$56 million**.  
> And you’re right. That money is collected, it goes to the state, the state redistributes it to school districts, **but not all of it. They actually keep a good amount of it.**  
> The problem is we have fought and fought and fought. And when you are only a couple legislators here in Central Texas and then every other legislator throughout the state of Texas, over 200, 300 of them collectively between the Senate and the House, **benefit from recapture** because their school districts are getting something. They’re not going to vote to have it be different than the way it already is.  
> That’s how you get things to the state. You have to have a majority… I promise you I we have been fighting… We’ve sued in 2017… We challenged it in 2019. This time around we’re pushing… but **it’s not going to change. It’s just not.**  
> Now… our approach kind of shifted for the **89th Legislature** and we said… Fine. I get that we’re not going to be able to move it… **let’s just fund public education.** Let’s raise the **basic student allotment**. Let’s figure out how to put billions of dollars into the school system and give districts the autonomy, when appropriate, to spend as they see fit… we choose to spend it on teachers.  
> There was some movement in the 89th… lots of the dollars were set asides for certain programs… confining how you spend your dollars. And at the end of the day, whenever it all nets out, it ended up being around a **$10 million increase for AISD** bottom line. Now teachers got raises, which is great… three raises in a row.  
> Another thing… the federal government. Title 1, Title 2, Title 3, and Title 4… Earlier this year I sent a letter… two, three, and four… frozen. I had to get **100 people in a room** and tell them that the source of funding used to pay their salary is frozen. And I’m not in a position… to carry them without really impacting our fund balance… multilingual education… all the things that we believe in.  
> I am trying to desperately move us to a place where we don’t have to rely on funding sources that are variable… unfortunately Austin in front of anything makes it to where almost everybody’s going to not vote for it.

**Staff add (Ali)** (`01:27:16`):

> And I’ll just add with this last legislative session, there were estimates that in order to get our funding up to **keep up with inflation** that we needed to get an extra **1,100 to 1,300 per student**. And what ended up coming was about came out to about **200 per student**. So that’s how— And the legislature wanted to celebrate. It was a big bill and it was a lot of money to schools, but it was nowhere near what it would cost just to keep up with the rising costs.

That is the allotment year, spoken at a middle school, not as a motion.
