Students reassigned by address to **Keeling, Lively, and Marshall** for the 2026–27 school year (`00:09:29`). Ally on TAP (`00:12:54`): before the consolidation plan released, the district said Martin would need a **turnaround plan** because of three consecutive unacceptable ratings. Majority to Keeling → joint Keeling–Martin TAP if the board approves (`00:13:20`–`00:13:53`). Appointed principal hires to TAP staffing criteria, prioritizing employees from both communities. Staff mix should roughly mirror student mix (example: 70% Keeling / 30% Martin). Staff not hired go to internal match fairs in February and March. “Our goal is to make sure that every staff person that wants to stay with Austin ISD has a place to land” (`00:15:01`). Consolidation is to “free up dollars” and reinvest “a large amount of those into the turnaround plan schools… about an **$800,000** investment” — extra counselors, APs, instructional coaches, content interventionists, parent support (`00:15:27`–`00:16:03`).

## Prepared comment-card questions (`00:16:17`–`00:29:19`)

**Eric R.:** What promises that Martin will reopen, with community input? (`00:16:32`)

Segura (`00:16:48`): “a very harmful moment.” The plan is trying to do several things at once:

1. Align East Austin feeder patterns. “For over 144 years… we have not had aligned feeder patterns. We have middle schools that go to three and four and five high schools. In other parts of town, that doesn’t happen” (`00:17:24`–`00:17:39`).
2. TEA turnaround. “We didn’t arrive at this plan thinking about that first. we went through the work and then layered in our ultimate response to TEA” (`00:18:25`). Martin is required to submit a TAP.
3. Consolidations. “We are running a deficit budget. Uh we cannot be at a moment where we are not able to fund the things that we have to fund” (`00:18:35`).
4. Stabilize wall-to-wall dual language. Emerging bilingual shares 14%, 21%, 22%, 44%; healthy is 50% (`00:19:09`).
5. Transfer policies “doing real harm” (`00:19:31`).

On reopen: majority of students to Keeling to “break the accountability chain.” After the move, “we will engage with community and we will build out what the future for Martin will be.” Pre-K–8 “is not a done deal” (`00:20:05`–`00:20:25`). He cannot give certainty of *what* it will be. He is looking for “what language can I use in the plan that allows our trustees to have confidence that there will be a school here in the future” (`00:21:07`). Typical new-school process is about two years: programming, hire principal, hire staff, bond package. “The board and I are committed to ensuring that there is a AISD public education… campus like on this site. Uh but I have to break the accountability chain. I just have to break it. Um if not, the risk is too great” (`00:22:12`).

**Stacy S.:** What feeder when repurposed? French: cannot answer until identity is known. For *this* reassignment: south of the river → Lively → Travis; Keeling → Eastside; Marshall → LBJ (`00:22:45`–`00:23:34`).

**Why take two modernized schools (Govalle and Sanchez)? Buses?** Ally: Sanchez proposed campuswide dual language (no attendance zone); Govalle campuswide Montessori (no zone); current students invited to opt in (`00:24:06`). Wall-to-wall at Sanchez also breaks an accountability chain; Govalle as districtwide Montessori; keeping a neighborhood school (he says Sanchez here) “allows us to not have to close another school” (`00:26:04`). Transportation to Sanchez: “the answer is yes we can” — considering consistency with Pickle, Odom, Wooten (`00:26:16`–`00:27:10`).

**Raul L.:** Bond funds at Martin? Steinport: construction “on pause”; identity first; “bond dollars to go with students”; a bond project at every AISD school (`00:27:27`–`00:28:21`). Ally: “the funding will stay with Martin” in some form (`00:28:46`). Segura: unique because “we are planning on having a school back at Martin”; other consolidations are “not likely to be a school at that location” (`00:28:57`).
