## Third Base after school (`[00:01:50]`–`[00:20:36]`)

Thirty minutes set aside. Travis County after-school report deferred to the June meeting. Written materials in the binders.

Martell: AISD after-school program more than 25 years old. Pre-this-year peak: seven elementary locations. After the pandemic: about 100 students at five campuses. District commitment to revitalize and expand to all elementary campuses in three phases:

- Phase 1, 2023–24 (current): **24 locations**.
- Phase 2, 2024–25: **16 more**.
- Phase 3, 2025–26: **38 locations**.

Tuition: “we are the cheapest program provider in our district. Currently it’s $275 per month” (`[00:04:13]`–`[00:04:19]`). Ratio 1:18. Staff about $20.67/hour, part-time. Wait list about 325. Lee Elementary: 100+ enrolled with ~60 on the wait list — “simply we” cannot add more because of space (`[00:06:36]`–`[00:06:43]`). Andrews, ODM, Winn added. Prime Time / ACE at Title I. Lee wait as a share of enrollment: 20–23%. Even campuses with three programs still have wait lists (Blandon named). “Austin is considered a childcare desert” (`[00:18:35]`–`[00:18:37]`).

Singh: after-school “especially critical… as we’re thinking about the impact of high quality programs on student outcomes um and with the state not really having a very positive um funding public education funding outlook to say the least” (`[00:19:48]`–`[00:20:04]`). She wants the **unmet need citywide**, not just one program’s wait list — “is it 10,000 students? Is it 5,000?” — including families who never sign up.

No vote. Expansion is a staff commitment already in phases.

## Parks and Recreation after school and summer (`[00:20:36]`–`[00:58:34]`)

Kimberly McNeely: city rec after-school about $140/week, city-supplemented (about $70 if families qualify). Summer about $560/week. Fifteen rec centers; about 600 enrolled against 720 capacity. About 1,400 on financial aid. Meals: ~90,000. TILT and Roving Leaders. Twenty-one green school parks. Wait lists from certain schools to certain rec centers because of space.

This is city programming, not AISD buses or AISD class size. No vote.

## Child-care tax exemption (`[00:58:34]`–`[01:05:06]`)

Working group: after Prop 2, “we will have both county and city tax exemptions for child care agencies.” Operators still apply at TCAD; “TCAD’s using the same exemption for the city and the county.” Home-based care is the harder case. If the exemption hits the landlord, the tenant should see “equivalent savings… in the form of a rent” reduction.

This is property-tax mechanics for child-care businesses, not AISD recapture or the district tax rate.

## Mental health (`[01:05:06]`–`~[01:18:13]`)

Twyla Williams: school counselors are not only academic counselors; they stay with the student from tier one relationships through higher need.

Dr. Kimberly Wilson: AISD mental-health initiative through Superintendent Matias and the board — “invest in student and staff well-being and mental wellness.” Multi-tiered system: professional learning, mental-health first aid, train staff over three to five years for early indicators “before it gets to the point where there’s a crisis.” Counselor role “was created for academic outcomes”; trauma work is to get students back into the classroom.

School-based mental-health centers: “we have 31 licensed mental health professionals” and centers “designed to be on every campus.” Later: “I am proud to say that we have about 94 school mental health centers” (`[01:18:11]`–`[01:18:13]`). Anderson portable is used as a site example in the Q&A stretch.

No special-education agreed-order update in this meeting. No TAP.

And the election is May 4th and virtually no one knows about” (`[01:34:30]`–`[01:34:46]`). School-revenue risk is named as appraisal politics, not recapture by name.
