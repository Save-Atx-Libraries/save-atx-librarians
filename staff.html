Staff

The state writes the entitlement. Austin ISD still decides which job leaves a campus. From the transcripts, the public’s list and the budget’s list are not the same list.

What people named

On April 23, speech-language pathologists called in a string: Shannon Donahue, Tracy Easley Carr at LBJ and Sadler Means, Sheila Ray at Allison, Zeb White and Kelly Keenan at Doss, Anne Jefferson at Highland Park. The point was the same. The district had just come off a TEA special-education order because those staff stayed. Cutting the stipend is a pay cut for the same work. They said that is how the 2020 loss of evaluation staff started, and how missed services start again.

On May 21, Aaron Boojhawon, choir at Bowie, said fine-arts colleagues were already being told they would not return, and that a “7 of 8” schedule — one less planning period — would hollow out programs students are required to take. On June 18, Lamar parents Dan Schmidt, Whitney, and Meredith named five fine-arts positions on the list, including an assistant band director and a theater director, and said choir at O. Henry was already closed.

Librarians were the other repeated name. A parent in April said a work session had floated about $2 million by converting librarian roles, and asked the board to find it somewhere else. In June, speakers said 16 of 23 campuses losing librarian time were Title I, and that some librarians had already been offered a split week before the vote.

What the board did with that

Special-education stipends, planning time, and the named fine-arts jobs did not get their own vote. They sat inside the budget. The budget passed.

Librarians did get amendments, late, on June 18. Trading the deputy superintendent and the ombudsman office for a librarian on every campus failed, 2–5–2. Using only the deputy superintendent’s pay to cover as many librarians as it would buy failed, 3–5–1. Keeping one full-time librarian per campus by taking the cost from fund balance — the superintendent described a move from about 13.1% to 13.01%, and said he would come back in August with a real offset — passed 6–3. Coverage put that patch near $1.1 million. On the tape the figures tossed around were about $900,000 and a salary that did not cover every campus.

That is a save, and it is a one-year save. It is not the order the April 30 summary of public comment asked for, which was central office before the campus.

The rest of the package

The adopted close-out was an $887 million operating budget after about $205 million in reductions. More than 500 positions were affected. Class sizes moved up. Planning time moved down. Neighborhood bus stops at middle and high school were replaced with hubs. In November 2025, before these transcripts, the board had already voted to close 10 campuses. Those closure roll calls are not in this set of tapes.

Names are as spoken in the captions, corrected where the mishear is obvious. Vote counts are the chair’s counts on the June 18, 2026 recording.
