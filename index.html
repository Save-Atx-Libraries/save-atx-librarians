A law can do one real thing and still leave the problem standing.

Recapture was written in 1993 because a poor tax base meant a poor school at the same tax rate. That part happened. It did not take an Austin ISD tax dollar and hand it to a named poor child, and it did not fill the rainy-day fund. It let the state count a local payment as its own share.

Those two facts sit in the same statute. Picking a side — kill the law, or leave it alone — skips the contract. The kids are still not learning at the rate the ratings demand. That is on the record. It is not a verdict that teachers wrote the formula.

What the minute at the microphone was for

From December 2025 through June 2026, people used a one-minute comment, in the room or by phone, to say the cut was not the fix. Speech therapists, a choir teacher, band parents, librarians, bilingual teachers. The board’s own April 30 work session listed the same priorities back to them: class size, planning time, nurses, counselors, librarians, assistants, and bilingual and special-education stipends, with central office first.

The votes did not follow that list. Most of them are a hand in the air after midnight, counted as “all those on the dais,” with no names. The budget passed 7–1–1. A try to trade central-office jobs for librarians failed. A later try to keep one full-time librarian per campus passed only by spending fund balance. The contract amendment for the superintendent passed with no opposition the same night.
