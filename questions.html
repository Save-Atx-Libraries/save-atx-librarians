Critical thinking · not a verdict

Why was the remaining tool set cuts, shuffles, and closures?

A serious problem can be true and still be treated with the wrong instrument. The questions below are for reading board video, public comment, and staff presentations side by side. They are not accusations. They are tests. If a test fails, write down what would falsify it next.

The common goal, stated so it can be checked

Polarized rooms still share a minimum. If this is not the goal, the board should say what is.

Working statement: Children in Austin public schools leave able to read, calculate, and live with other people — including people they will disagree with — without burning out the adults who teach them. Closures, recapture, and class-size math are means. They are not that goal.

Ask of every slide in a board deck: does this move a child toward that statement, or does it move a spreadsheet toward a deadline? Both can matter. They are not the same. A $181 million hole is real. So is a teacher asked, in June, to take more students, lose a planning period, and “ready” children for a society the adults in the room cannot describe in one sentence.

Why wait until the only moves left were subtraction?

Texas grades campuses. Consecutive failure at a campus can escalate to a district takeover. That clock is public. So is enrollment decline. So is the frozen allotment. Sitting still is also a decision. The question is what the meetings show about when urgency appeared.

  1. Q1
    When did “this campus is failing” become “the district must cut”?

    Search last year’s videos for the first date a trustee or superintendent names Burnet, Webb, or another TAP campus as a district survival problem rather than a campus instruction problem. If that language only arrives with the deficit letter, the failure was allowed to become a legal trigger. If it arrives earlier, what resource followed it — and did that resource stay?

  2. Q2
    Why do turnaround costs eat closure savings?

    Chronicle reporting: about $21 million from eleven closures, about $17 million already committed to state-mandated turnaround. The superintendent called it “almost a one to one.” Why close a neighborhood school to fund a compliance plan on another campus? What would a meeting transcript show about alternatives that were not scored?

  3. Q3
    Why were families and staff told late?

    Campus allocations and librarian-share notices landed near the last week of school. Why does a multi-year enrollment slide (AISD’s own recapture table, 2014–15 through 2026) still produce a May surprise? Is the delay from changing state numbers — or from waiting until the balanced-budget deadline made dissent expensive?

  4. Q4
    Why is “shuffle teachers / grow class size” the default labor plan?

    112 certified staff moved. Secondary planning time converted into 150 positions. Elementary ratios at Band 3–4 campuses go to 24:1 and 27:1. What theory of learning is that? Who, on the dais, said it would raise achievement at the same campuses the state already says are failing? Quote the timestamp.

  5. Q5
    Why did public comment have to rescue librarians twice?

    April: community fills the room to keep librarians. District had said they would be spared. June: 11.5 FTE back on the cut list. June 18: amendment restores them onto fund balance. If the board already heard the argument, what failed in the administration’s scoring so that the same line returned? That is a process anomaly, not a feeling.

  6. Q6
    Why do buses and attendance get treated as separate from “enrollment crisis”?

    Trustee Singh said hubs give her pause because of attendance and enrollment. Texas pays on ADA. Cutting the ride can cut the dollar. Did any presentation model lost ADA against the $5.1 million hub savings? If not, the model is incomplete on the district’s own funding rule.

  7. Q7
    Why is Austin’s map treated as background rather than cause?

    1928 plan, one-way busing, 2019 East Austin closures, 2026 consolidations. Under-enrollment was predicted by the way the city stacked small segregated schools. When staff say “efficiency,” ask whether they mean undoing a segregation footprint or repeating it as real estate. See Austin.

  8. Q8
    Why is inflation someone else’s job while class size is a principal’s job?

    The allotment lagged ~$1,500 per student. HB 2 added $55. Campus adults absorb the remainder. Which meeting, if any, asked the board to send a single, repeated, numbered request to the Legislature instead of a new internal cut list? Count the mentions of “basic allotment” versus “vacancy savings.”

  9. Q9
    Why are the superintendent contract and the property sales not in the same packet as class size?

    Base $362,250, supplemental retirement $36,750, reimbursed business travel including rental car, and on the order of $60 million in one-time property monetization sit in public documents. Recapture is the state’s skim; it does not make those local lines invisible. Ask for the reimbursements and the sale assumptions on the record. A nickname is not an audit.

  10. Q10
    What is being asked of a teacher that no feed can do?

    More students, less planning time, a June reassignment, and “ready these kids” for a polarized country. If the common goal is still a child who can read and live with other people, say that in the meeting. Scrolling the disaster is not the same work.

How to use a meeting without turning it into a rant

A board video is a primary source. Treat it like one. Timestamp. Name the speaker role (public, trustee, superintendent, staff). Write the claim in one sentence. Write what would prove it false. Then search other dates for the same claim.

  • Recurring theme — same phrase, many months (recapture, enrollment, “hard choices”).
  • Anomaly — a promise that reverses without a new number (librarians; “campuses won’t carry the brunt”).
  • Silence — a number that never appears (ADA loss from hubs; inflation gap vs $55).
  • Public vs dais — do public comments name a campus reality that never enters the slide?

The local reader on this site does that work on your files. Nothing is uploaded. You point it at the .txt subtitles you already have.

A question that is not optional

If the common goal is children ready for a shared future, why is the operational plan to give teachers more children, less time, and a June reassignment — at the same moment the state can take the district for academic failure? Those two facts can sit in one year. They cannot both be a strategy unless someone explains the theory. Ask for the theory, on the record, with a timestamp.
