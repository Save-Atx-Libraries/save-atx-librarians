What the year said, counted.

32 meetings from Desktop/New Folder (one English caption file each). Times are from the files. Speech-to-text errors are in the source. This folder is the 2025 consolidation / turnaround year. The June 2026 librarian-cut package is not in it.

Determination

In these captions, AISD spent the year treating TEA takeover risk as the master problem and closing / consolidating schools as the master tool. Librarians, nurses, and planning time were the district’s identity script — repeated while the same campuses were listed. Recapture and inflation were named, rarely. They were not the vote.

That is a description of what got airtime and what got a motion. It is not a claim that takeover risk was fake, or that every closure was a lie. It is a claim about which problem the room was built to solve.

Closure / TAP Dominate every long board night and every campus meeting. Nov. 20 voting file alone is dense with both.
Recapture A handful of real hits: ~$700–715M on the dais; $714M + Robin Hood at Bedichek. Not an action item.
Inflation One clean hit: Bedichek, ~1:27 — needed $1,100–$1,300 per student, got about $200.
Class size Almost absent as a fight. The later 2026 ratio change is a different season.

The year, in order

  1. 2023–24
    Joint subcommittee (in this folder, dated April 21, 2023 and April 1, 2024)

    Takeover language is already operational: consecutive campus failure, conservatorship “one step below a takeover,” special-ed audit path. The later 2025 fear did not start in September 2025.

  2. Sep 18, 2025
    Information session

    Public asks a moratorium on consolidations: TEA wants teachers, leadership, curriculum, families — “not buildings.” Winn Montessori asks for time under turnaround discretion. Another speaker: no academic vision, facilities still moving. The board does not pause the process.

  3. Sep 25
    Regular voting

    Staff: protect planning time; keep “supportive librarians.” Same night: operating at a deficit that must be “made up.” Identity and hole in one meeting.

  4. Oct 9–10
    Special meeting + consolidation briefing

    Public: “number one priority is to avoid a TEA takeover, but closing schools is obeying in advance.” Staff: recapture ~$700M sits inside a 1.6–1.9B “overall” figure. Briefing: aim toward 85% utilization, currently 76%; close as few as possible. Staff later the same cycle: “We retained librarians. We retained registered nurses.” And: “We haven’t reduced our librarians.”

  5. Oct 21 – Nov 12
    Campus meetings (Martin, Sunset Valley, Barrington, Dawson, Palm, Maplewood, Widen, Bryker Woods, Becker, Bedichek, Winn, Oak Springs, Ridgetop, Blackshear)

    Same district package: TAP inheritance, maps, “protect librarians / planning time,” you will still have a librarian at the receiving school. Public keeps bringing recapture, surplus, and “show the savings.” Maplewood: old habit of trading a librarian for an instructional coach. Palm ~00:37: “We’ve decided to keep librarians… above and beyond what the state [requires].” Dawson: librarians and nurses as extras the state does not require — i.e. already framed as optional.

  6. Oct 29–30
    Work session, hearing, vote

    Public at work session: no proof closing saves a dollar; attrition + transport + recapture can cost AISD; closing “good schools will push more kids out.” Oct 30 vote night: operating ~$992M, recapture estimated $715M, cash/fund balance low; programming, compensation, planning periods, 116 campuses “overwhelming us.”

  7. Nov 3
    Bedichek (clearest finance night in the community set)

    Public: ~$700M / $714M to Robin Hood; $56M more; $24B state surplus. “Let’s raise the basic student allotment.” Staff: inflation catch-up needed $1,100–$1,300 per student; session delivered about $200. That is the formula year, spoken at a middle school, not as a board motion.

  8. Nov 6–13
    Information session + TAP video

    Board night is again TAP + consolidation. Superintendent video: turnaround plans required, to be submitted. Closures and TAPs are one compliance machine. Oak Springs ~1:16: “We protected planning time… because that’s who we are.”

  9. Nov 20
    Hearing + regular vote — item 14.3

    Public, repeatedly: vote no on 14.3 (closures / program moves; dual-language “closing Ridgetop” appears in captions as “closing rich”). A speaker: a trustee had wanted last-round savings and enrollment loss before another yes; “these numbers will never be forthcoming. And yet we expect a yes vote tonight.” Late: 14.3 as amended carries 6–3 (~05:58).

What that determines

1. The room was built around takeover, not around the allotment. “Avoid TEA takeover” is the phrase that organizes both staff decks and public fear. Closures are sold as the way to feed turnaround plans and utilization. A speaker on Oct 9 called that “obeying in advance.” Whether they were right is a later test. That they had to say it means the vote was already aimed at TEA, not at Chapter 49.

2. Librarians were a brand before they were a cut. Fall 2025 captions are full of “we retained / we haven’t reduced / we’re protecting / we love librarians.” Six months later the 2026 deficit plan put 11.5 librarian FTE on a cut list, then the June 18 amendment put them on fund balance. The captions make that sequence a reversal of a stated identity, not a surprise invented in June.

3. Recapture was known and not voted. $700–715M is said on the dais (Oct 9, Oct 30). $714M and “raise the basic allotment” are said at Bedichek. Inflation math is said once. None of those became item 14.3. Item 14.3 was buildings and programs.

4. The last closure round’s proof was requested and, in public comment, refused. Nov 20 ~00:15: savings and enrollment loss “will never be forthcoming,” then a 6–3 yes. If those numbers exist, they were not the condition of the vote. If they do not exist, the utilization story is un-audited in public.

5. Planning time was promised in the same breath as the later cut list. Protect planning time: Sep 25, Sunset Valley, Oak Springs, Oct 9. June 2026 reporting: planning time reduced on some campuses. Same pattern as librarians: identity, then the remainder.

Limits of this read

Auto-captions. Names and school names scramble (Winn, Widen, Ridgetop). Cue counts are inflated by triplicate SRT lines; the ratio still holds: closure/TAP speech swamps recapture/inflation.

This is not 2026. Do not treat a fall 2025 “we keep librarians” line as the June 18 amendment. Treat it as the promise the June plan had to break or walk back.
