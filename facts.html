Facts

These are not four separate stories. The Legislature sets a low base. The formula treats Austin land as the wealth. Recapture sends the tax money into the state’s school account. The board closes what is left by cutting the people in the buildings.

When it started

Senate Bill 7 was signed May 31, 1993, one day before a court deadline that would have cut off state school aid. The Legislative Reference Library lists it as effective immediately. The first year anyone sent money was 1994: 34 districts, $131 million, statewide. Austin ISD was not the target then. The law was aimed at districts whose dirt could out-tax everyone else.

Newspapers called it Robin Hood. The bill did not. “Rich” means taxable property per student, not family income. In 2025–26, 48.4% of Austin ISD students were classified economically disadvantaged inside a district the formula calls property-wealthy.

What the dollar does

The check does not travel to a named campus, and it does not go into the rainy-day fund. The Comptroller books recapture as Foundation School Program money. That reduces what the state has to put in from its own revenue. Equity — similar money at a similar tax rate — is not the same sentence as adequacy — enough money to teach the child. Texas did the first and talked as if it had done the second.

Austin ISD’s published cumulative is about $8.3 billion from 2000–01 through 2024–25. There is no district total on that page that starts in 1994. The adopted payment for 2025–26 is $715,523,373, just under half of a $1.70 billion adopted expenditure budget. Statewide, districts together have sent about $45.5 billion since 1994. Austin has been the largest single payer for nearly 20 years.

The base dollar

House Bill 3 set the basic allotment at $6,160 in 2019. House Bill 2 added $55, so the working base this biennium is $6,215. Special education, bilingual, and compensatory weights multiply that base. A base that barely moves shrinks every weight in real dollars. Consumer prices from 2019 to 2025 would have put $6,160 near $7,756. That is not a law. Bills that would have moved the allotment toward $7,500 died in the 2025 session.

The other account

The Economic Stabilization Fund held $27.5 billion on June 30, 2026. The constitutional cap for the 2026–27 biennium is $26.9 billion in the Comptroller’s bond disclosure, so the fund is over the cap. Oil and gas severance taxes fill it. Past the cap, extra transfers stop. The Legislature can spend the fund: a three-fifths vote if a shortfall is certified, or two-thirds for any purpose. Austin ISD cannot.
