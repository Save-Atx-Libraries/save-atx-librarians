Glossary

What these words mean.

Board packets and meeting captions use shorthand. This page says what that shorthand is, in ordinary English. It is not a second argument. It is a dictionary for the pages that already hold the numbers.

Recapture
A Texas rule that takes local property-tax money from a district labeled “property-wealthy” and sends it to the state. Austin ISD’s adopted recapture for FY2025–26 is about $715.5 million. See the recapture bill.
Chapter 49
The section of the Texas Education Code that is recapture. It used to be called Chapter 41. Same machine, new number.
“Robin Hood”
Newspaper nickname for recapture: take from “rich” districts, give to “poor” ones. The legal test is taxable property value per student, not whether a child’s family is rich. Austin can pay recapture and still have nearly half of students classified economically disadvantaged.
Property-wealthy
TEA’s label for a district whose taxable land and buildings, divided by students, sit above a state wealth cap. It is a real-estate ratio. It is not a description of the children in the building.
Basic allotment
The state’s base dollar per student in the school-finance formula. Set at $6,160 in 2019. Extra funding for poverty, language, and disability is a multiplier on this number. When the base freezes, those extras freeze in real terms. See the allotment froze.
GYIA
Guaranteed Yield Increment Adjustment. House Bill 2’s $55 add-on for 2025–27. It is not an inflation index. Statutory basic allotment remains $6,160; with GYIA the working figure is $6,215.
TEA
Texas Education Agency. The state office that grades campuses A–F, can close a school or replace a board, and has to accept a failing campus’s turnaround paperwork.
A–F ratings
TEA’s letter grades for campuses and districts. An F is “Unacceptable.” Several F years in a row trigger state intervention. Letter grades measure a test-and-accountability clock. They do not measure whether a child still has a librarian or a neighborhood bus stop.
UA / consecutive failure
Unacceptable. A campus in a “UA chain” has failed TEA’s rating for consecutive years. After enough years the commissioner shall close the school or install a board of managers — a state takeover of the district.
TAP
Turnaround Plan. A document TEA requires for a campus that has failed its ratings. It can include extra staff, a restart, or closing the building and sending students elsewhere. In 2025–26 AISD tied TAPs to consolidations. Closure savings were estimated around $21 million; about $17 million was already committed to those turnaround costs.
Takeover / conservatorship / board of managers
State intervention. Conservatorship is one step below replacing the elected board. A board of managers is the commissioner putting state appointees in charge. AISD treated that risk as the reason to close schools and cut campus time on TEA’s calendar.
Utilization
How full a building is, as a percentage of seats. Staff aimed at about 85% from about 76%. Empty seats were compared to the Moody Center. Utilization counts rooms, not whether a child can get to school.
ADA
Average daily attendance. Texas pays on who shows up, not on who is enrolled. Cutting neighborhood bus stops can lower ADA and therefore lower state aid — the same formula the district says is in crisis.
Hubs
The 2026 transportation cut: secondary students lose neighborhood stops and meet the bus at an elementary campus instead. Scored as about $5.1 million saved. Lost attendance was not scored in the public materials this site cites.
Fund balance
Reserves — money already in the bank, not a new state dollar. The June 18 librarian amendment parks those jobs on fund balance until replacement cuts are named. That is a one-year hold, not a formula fix.
VATRE
Voter-approval tax-rate election. Austin’s 2024 Proposition A raised the maintenance-and-operations rate. In a recapture district, most of a local tax increase still leaves for the state. District materials: about $171 million extra levy, about $130 million recaptured, about $41 million net to AISD.
Band 1 / Band 3–4
AISD’s grouping of campuses by need and turnaround status. Band 1 (turnaround) kept smaller class-size ratios. Band 3–4 elementaries went to 24:1 in grades 2–4 and 27:1 in grade 5. Those rooms still have children.
Item 14.3
The November 20, 2025 board vote on consolidations and program moves. It carried 6–3 after more than two hours of public “vote no.” Recapture, inflation, and the basic allotment do not appear in that voting file.
What a student actually gets (called “intrinsic” on the meetings page)
The school day: a known adult, planning time, a librarian, a neighborhood stop, dual language, a counselor who is not also covering three other jobs. That is what a child receives. It is not a TEA form.
What the state grades (called “extrinsic” on the meetings page)
Letter grades, TAP paperwork, utilization percentages, fund-balance floors, recapture cash dates, TEA upload deadlines. Those are real pressures. They are not the same as a funded classroom.
FTE
Full-time equivalent. 11.5 librarian FTE meant 23 small campuses would share a librarian — half-time at each — until the June 18 amendment put those jobs back, temporarily, on reserves.
Stipend
Extra pay for extra work, here bilingual and special-education stipends. The 2026 package cut them. The formula still counts those students as higher-need.
Planning time
The period a teacher has to prepare, grade, and meet families. The 2026 package converted some of that time into instructional positions (about 150). Less planning, more students in the room.
Economically disadvantaged
TEA’s poverty classification. 48.4% of AISD students. Compensatory education funding sits on the basic allotment, so a frozen base is a frozen extra for those students.
Lone Star Governance
A TEA-backed board monitoring routine. On some nights the dais moved from public comment into tracking a sixth-grade math goal. Process. Not a second budget option.
