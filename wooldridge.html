Congratulations · 2025–26

Congratulations to the library, former Principal Juan Cardenas, and all the teachers and staff who keep Wooldridge running.

Despite a recapture system the state built, an allotment that froze, and a local budget year of closures and campus cuts, Wooldridge Elementary went from an F to a B. In a year this site documents as overcast — the recapture bill, staffing taken off campuses, librarians named on a public cut list, then restored after people showed up — last year’s library, former Principal Juan Cardenas, and the teachers and staff who keep that school running were a clear day.

Congratulations. F to B in 2025–26. The A–F system is the measure the state uses. Last year’s staff met it, on a campus the rest of the district’s year did not treat as a success story.

For the 2025–26 staff

Shout-out to the library, former Principal Juan Cardenas, and all the teachers and staff who keep that school running. You changed a campus the state had rated F, with the students who walk onto Norseman Terrace. Last year you earned a B — a bright result in a year the rest of these pages walk through as formula pressure and local cuts.

What followed that year

The dates, then the questions they raise

In December 2025 the superintendent named a different principal for 2026–27, while last year’s staff was still finishing the year that produced the B.

Dated facts

Nov. 2025: board papers put Wooldridge on a district-managed ACE restart — restaff — off three prior F ratings, and close Barrington with students zoned to Guerrero-Thompson or Wooldridge (board night: about 83 to Wooldridge). Dec. 9, 2025: Nick Martinez, then principal of Wooten, appointed Wooldridge principal for 2026–27. Wooten was F in 2025; KVUE’s 2026 F list still includes Wooten. Aug. 2026: the B for last year posts. The replacement had already been named.

Still unanswered

Why move a principal from a campus that was failing onto a campus that had just earned a B? ACE restart lets that principal hire. How many of last year’s Wooldridge staff returned, and how many came from Wooten? Sources.

Questions the dates actually raise

  • If a principal is told in December he will not be the principal next year, what is last year’s remaining work, for him and for the whole staff who still have to teach it?
  • Why keep leading a campus through the year that becomes an F-to-B if the next-year job is already assigned to someone else?
  • What does “not coming back” do to a staff that is still in the building — library, classrooms, office — while the letter grade is still being earned?
  • Why move the Wooten principal onto Wooldridge for 2026–27 — a campus that had just earned a B under last year’s staff with former Principal Cardenas — when Wooten itself was F in 2025 and remains on the 2026 F list?
  • ACE restart lets the new principal hire. How many of last year’s Wooldridge staff returned, and how many came from Wooten with the new principal?

More kids. A restaff. Why touch the library at all?

Board materials for 2026–27 send Barrington children to Guerrero-Thompson or Wooldridge by address — about 83 Barrington students named for Wooldridge, 279 for Guerrero-Thompson. That is more kids in this building the same year last year’s whole staff is not simply left in place. ACE restart means the staff reapply. District-wide, the spring budget tried to share librarians at campuses under 400 that were not on improvement plans, then the June 18 amendment put every campus librarian back, on fund balance. Wooldridge was over 400 and already on a turnaround paper, so it was not on that shared-librarian list. The question is still the district’s instinct in the same year:

  • Why add children to a campus whose last-year staff with former Principal Cardenas just went F to B — and restaff that building instead of keeping the people who earned the letter?
  • If the honor for a B is more kids plus a restart, what happens to adult time per child: library, classrooms, office, cafeteria?
  • Why was shrinking libraries the district’s opening move anywhere, in the same budget that asked this staff to absorb a closed neighbor?
  • If a full staff and a library are what last year used to get a B, why would the next year be designed around fewer familiar adults and more students?

Why was the 2026–27 plan around a campus that had just earned a B more students and less of last year’s staff?
