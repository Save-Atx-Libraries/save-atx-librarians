Last year · 2025–26 · the whole staff

Wooldridge: last year’s whole staff, with Cardenas. You are badass.

This page is not the recapture chapter. It is a recognition page for one campus year. The 2025–26 staff — all of them, with Principal Juan Cardenas — took Wooldridge Elementary from an F to a B on the TEA report card. That letter belongs to the people who were in the building last year. Library. Teachers. Aides. Office. Cafeteria. Custodians. Counselor. Assistant principal. Instructional coach. Principal. The whole staff.

Last year. Not a five-year average. Not a downtown slide. F to B. The whole staff with Cardenas did the work the A–F system claims to measure. Last year they measured it.

F → B Wooldridge Elementary on the 2026 TEA ratings, covering the 2025–26 school year — last year. Statesman, Aug. 20, 2026 · KVUE 2026 list
Whole staff With Principal Juan Cardenas, named on the district’s own 2025–26 turnaround and campus improvement papers. AISD TAP / CIP
Last year The year that was still being taught when the district named someone else for 2026–27. AISD, Dec. 10, 2025

This is the shout-out.

If you were on that staff last year: this is for you. You moved a campus the state had labeled failing. You did it with the kids who actually walk in the door on Norseman Terrace, not with a press conference. SOLATX does not speak for AISD. It can still say the obvious. Last year’s whole staff with Cardenas was badass.

The rating is a public letter. The credit is local. Read it next to how campuses took the load and the librarian bandage if you want the district-wide story. This page stays with one year and one staff.

On the record, without a novel

What the files show. Then the questions.

The appointment is public. A termination letter is not in the files we have. “Fired” is the wrong word here. Forced out of the next year is the shape of the memo: in December 2025 the superintendent named a different principal for 2026–27, while last year’s staff was still finishing the year that produced the B.

Dated facts

Nov. 2025: board papers put Wooldridge on a district-managed ACE restart — restaff — off three prior F ratings, and close Barrington with students zoned to Guerrero-Thompson or Wooldridge (board night: about 83 to Wooldridge). Dec. 9, 2025: Nick Martinez, then principal of Wooten, appointed Wooldridge principal for 2026–27. Wooten was F in 2025; KVUE’s 2026 F list still includes Wooten. Aug. 2026: the B for last year posts. The replacement had already been named.

What we will not invent

We do not have a private personnel file. We will not write a pension plot, a secret deal, or a motive we cannot source. Those make rage. They do not make a record. If something else is in an open-records letter, put it on sources when it exists.

Questions the dates actually raise

  • If a principal is told in December he will not be the principal next year, what is last year’s remaining work, for him and for the whole staff who still have to teach it?
  • Why keep leading a campus through the year that becomes an F-to-B if the next-year job is already assigned to someone else?
  • What does “not coming back” do to a staff that is still in the building — library, classrooms, office — while the letter grade is still being earned?
  • Why is the named replacement the principal of a campus that failed the year before, and that is still on the 2026 F list?
  • If ACE restart means teachers reapply, which of last year’s whole staff was asked to re-earn a seat they had just proven?

More kids. A restaff. Why touch the library at all?

Board materials for 2026–27 send Barrington children to Guerrero-Thompson or Wooldridge by address — about 83 Barrington students named for Wooldridge, 279 for Guerrero-Thompson. That is more kids in this building the same year last year’s whole staff is not simply left in place. ACE restart means the staff reapply. District-wide, the spring budget tried to share librarians at campuses under 400 that were not on improvement plans, then the June 18 amendment put every campus librarian back, on fund balance. Wooldridge was over 400 and already on a turnaround paper, so it was not on that shared-librarian list. The question is still the district’s instinct in the same year:

  • Why add children to a campus whose last-year staff, with Cardenas, just went F to B — and restaff that building instead of keeping the people who earned the letter?
  • If the honor for a B is more kids plus a restart, what happens to adult time per child: library, classrooms, office, cafeteria?
  • Why was shrinking libraries the district’s opening move anywhere, in the same budget that asked this staff to absorb a closed neighbor?
  • If a full staff and a library are what last year used to get a B, why would the next year be designed around fewer familiar adults and more students?

Those are questions for the district and the board, in public. They are not a story we will finish for them. We are not writing that AISD closed Wooldridge’s library. We are asking why the plan around this campus was more kids and a smaller claim on last year’s staff.
