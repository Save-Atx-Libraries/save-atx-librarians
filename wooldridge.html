Congratulations, Wooldridge. Dedication did what the formula did not.

This site is recapture and a state that prices Austin as property-rich while the weights for economically disadvantaged, bilingual, and special-education students multiply against a frozen allotment. Wooldridge is the campus that still could: last year’s staff, former Principal Juan Cardenas, a library open all year, an F to a B. They showed up without district support. The question is what these schools could accomplish if they were properly funded across the board. See the recapture bill and the freeze.

Wooldridge · B 2026 TEA rating for the 2025–26 school year — last year. The year before had been an F. Statesman, Aug. 20, 2026 · KVUE 2026 list
Wooten · F Same 2026 ratings, same last year. That is the campus the new Wooldridge principal came from. KVUE 2026 F list · TEA 2025 report card, overall 49
Dec. 9, 2025 Mid-year: Cardenas still finishing 2025–26, already named out for 2026–27. AISD announcement, Dec. 10, 2025

They still could. The district did not put money behind that.

On Dec. 9, 2025, while 2025–26 was still in session, Superintendent Segura appointed Nick Martinez, then principal of Wooten Elementary, as Wooldridge principal for 2026–27. Former Principal Juan Cardenas stayed through the end of last year and led the campus that posted the B. He already knew the next-year job was assigned to someone else.

Wooten’s rating for that same last year is an F. Wooldridge’s is a B. About 83 Barrington students were zoned into Wooldridge for 2026–27 as Barrington closed. ACE restart means last year’s staff reapply; the new principal hires. For 2026–27 the school is not even at 1412 Norseman Terrace. AISD swung it into the closed Barrington campus at 400 Cooper Drive while the home site is rebuilt with 2022 bond money. That is an older building on a high-traffic North Austin corridor — I-35 / Rundberg country — after the year they earned a B.

Questions those dates raise

  • What does it cost a campus to finish an F-to-B year after December, when the principal already knows he is gone at year’s end?
  • Why bring the Wooten principal to Wooldridge for 2026–27, when Wooten scored an F last year and Wooldridge scored a B?
  • How many of last year’s Wooldridge staff returned, and how many came from Wooten with Martinez?
  • Why add Barrington children to a campus that had just earned a B, then restaff it instead of keeping the people who earned the letter?

They overcame the odds. On the report card that is a major achievement. Properly funded across the board — hires, wages, teaching tools — the question is what that same staff could have done next. What they got was not that. ACE restart: reapply; the new principal hires. Cardenas out. More students from Barrington. A swing into the closed Barrington building. The new principal came from Wooten, which printed an F the same year they printed a B.

The B was dedication, not a surplus. Staff who buy materials themselves are working after recapture: 46.3 cents of each FY2024–25 general-fund dollar went to the state, and the extra weights for need still sit on a frozen allotment. The rainy-day fund held $27.5 billion on June 30, 2026. That pile did not become wages or tools in this building. See the recapture bill.
